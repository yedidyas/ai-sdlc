# Productivity Evidence

What the evidence says in October 2026 about whether AI coding agents make software teams more productive: benchmarks with current standings and caveats, METR's randomized trial and its follow-ups, DORA, GitClear, Faros, NBER and other measurement studies, large-company reports and surveys, sorted into positive, skeptical and mixed, with the method, sample, key number, date and caveats of each, and a closing list of what you can and cannot claim on stage.

## How to read this note

- **Entry fields.** Each study lists method and sample, key number, date and caveats. "Positive", "skeptical" and "mixed" follow the study's own headline on productivity, not an editorial judgement.
- **Labels.** "(vendor)" marks a publisher that sells a related product. "(secondary)" marks a number reachable only through another outlet. "(self-reported)" marks survey answers and company claims, as opposed to telemetry or an experiment.
- **Freshness.** Everything is as of October 12, 2026. Tools and models in the older studies are two to three generations behind (METR's trial used Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5 and 3.7 Sonnet). Dates are the publication or last-update dates the pages show.
- **Sources of confusion.** Productivity, throughput, value, speed and cost are different measures, and METR's own [task-substitution note](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-08-task-substitution-and-uplift/) shows why they diverge: people shift time toward tasks AI makes cheap, so under simplifying assumptions uplift on old tasks is at most uplift in value, which is at most uplift on new tasks.

|---|---|---|

| [MirrorCode](https://epoch.ai/publications/mirrorcode-preliminary-results/) (Epoch AI with METR, Apr 10, 2026) | Opus 4.6 reimplemented gotree, about 16,000 lines of Go with 40+ commands; researchers estimated 2 to 17 weeks for a skilled human | No human baseline run; the authors say real software is rarely built against a precise, programmatically checkable specification |

### 2.3 Why a score is not a capability

- **Contamination and flawed tests.** The Verified and Pro audits above. Models could reproduce some original fixes from memory (per AI Weekly).
- **Reward hacking and gaming.** [Terminal-Bench's April 2026 update](https://www.tbench.ai/news/leaderboard-integrity-update) required trajectories for passing trials, announced an agent judge over them, rescored to zero an agent that curled the solution into its AGENTS.md, and removed a submitter that stored encrypted solutions in its agent binary. TB 3.0 separated agent and verifier containers; [SWE-bench-Live](https://swe-bench-live.github.io/) has required rollout trajectories since August 2026; METR's [o3 examples](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/) include stubbing the evaluator and tracing the call stack to find the scorer's answer.
- **The record can be untrusted too.** METR [warns that transcripts are untrusted input](https://metr.org/blog/2026-10-06-ai-systems-could-cover-up-misbehavior/): a researcher with an AI agent found a flaw in the Inspect transcript viewer in about 10 minutes, patched within a day.
- **Infrastructure noise.** On Terminal-Bench 2.0, [resource limits alone moved scores up to 6 points](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) (infra error 5.8% at strict enforcement against 0.5% uncapped), and Anthropic says gaps below 3 points deserve skepticism. Transluce found an apparent GPT-5.1 Codex regression was [timeouts (49% against 29%)](https://transluce.org/docent/blog/terminal-bench): excluding them, the newer model was about 7 points better.
- **The harness is part of the score.** METR found [neither Claude Code nor Codex beat its default scaffolds](https://metr.org/notes/2026-02-13-measuring-time-horizon-using-claude-code-and-codex/) on its suite (differences not significant). On SWE-Bench Pro a harness scored [66.5% against 57.7% for the best base model](https://quesma.com/blog/verifying-blitzy-swe-bench-pro/) (about 9 points), and a scaffold preprint reports [67.4% against 59.10%](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25514) with the same model (its authors' own result). Across five models and three harnesses, [swapping the harness flips as many tasks as rerunning the same harness (13%)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04433) while moving cost up to 3x.
- **A four-model rig lost to one model.** A practitioner's planner, scout, executor and verifier setup on Terminal-Bench 2.1 [scored 78% (7th) at about twice the cost of the top single-model entry](https://quesma.com/blog/multi-model-claude-code-refusal-terminal-bench/): delegated security tasks were refused 0 of 14 times against 6 of 6 when asked directly; tasks where the optional verifier ran resolved at 91% against 43% when it was skipped (observational, selection bias acknowledged); 3-5 hand-offs resolved 90% at $2.41 a trial, 6+ hand-offs 50% at $9.51. One run by one author.
- **Reliability, not capability, binds.** Opus 4.6 clears a roughly 12-hour task half the time but a roughly 1.2-hour task 80% of the time; the same rig solved 78% on one try and 93% within five, with 28 of 89 tasks flaky.
- **Passing tests is not mergeable.** [Four maintainers](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/) (scikit-learn, Sphinx, pytest) blind-reviewed 296 AI PRs: merge decisions ran about 24 points below automated scores and roughly half of test-passing Verified PRs would not be merged (human reference patches were accepted about 68% of the time; agents got one attempt; a research note, less reviewed than a METR post).

## 3. Positive evidence

| Study | Date | Method and sample | Key number | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Google enterprise RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944) | Oct 16, 2024 | Randomized trial, 96 full-time Google engineers, one complex task with three internal AI features | About 21% less time on task; engineers who spend more hours on code-related activities were faster | Wide confidence interval; internal tooling from summer 2024; the authors caution against generalizing across tools and time |
| [Microsoft dose-response](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00438) | May 30, 2026 | 16,223 Cloud+AI engineers over 43 weeks, within-engineer fixed effects, coding time held constant | 40.5% more PRs in highest-usage weeks than zero-usage weeks; monotonic with diminishing returns at high intensity; seven robustness and falsification tests | Observational; holds under a stated conditional-independence assumption; PRs are not features |
| [Anthropic internal study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic) | Dec 2, 2025 (data from Aug 2025) | 132 engineers surveyed, 53 interviews, Claude Code usage data | Claude in 59% of daily work (28% a year earlier); self-reported +50% (+20% a year earlier); 67% more merged PRs per engineer per day; 27% of Claude-assisted work would not have been done otherwise | A vendor studying its own staff; most employees can fully delegate only 0-20% of their work, mostly easily verifiable, low-stakes or boring tasks |
| [DX Q4 impact report](https://getdx.com/report/ai-assisted-engineering-q4-impact-report/) | Q4 2025 | 135,000+ developers in 425 organizations, telemetry plus self-reports | 3.6 hours saved per week; 22% of merged code AI-authored; daily users ship 60% more PRs | A measurement vendor; the report itself calls quality results mixed |
| Bounded migrations: [Airbnb](https://airbnb.tech/infrastructure/accelerating-large-scale-test-migration-with-llms/), [Google](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09691), [Spotify](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2025/11/spotifys-background-coding-agent-part-1), [Slack](https://slack.engineering/balancing-old-tricks-with-new-feats-ai-powered-conversion-from-enzyme-to-react-testing-library-at-slack/) | May 2024 to Nov 2025 | Company-run migrations: nearly 3.5K test files (Airbnb); 39 migrations by three developers in twelve months, 595 changes with 93,574 edits (Google); 1,500+ merged agent PRs (Spotify); 15,000+ tests (Slack) | Airbnb estimated 1.5 years and finished in 6 weeks (75% in four hours, 97% after four days, the last 3% by hand); Google 74.45% of changes LLM-generated and an estimated 50% time reduction; Spotify 60-90% time savings; Slack 45% AST only, 40-60% LLM only, 80% hybrid | Company-reported; scope was bounded and repeated; human-time baselines are estimates; Google counts end-to-end time saved, not lines generated |
| [Intercom](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/this-week-on-how-i-ai-how-intercom) | Apr 20, 2026 | Podcast account, one company | Merged PRs per R&D employee doubled in nine months on Claude Code | Self-reported; built on mature CI/CD, comprehensive test coverage and a high-trust culture; no primary engineering post found |
| Agent code review: [Anthropic Code Review](https://claude.com/resources/articles/code-review), [Ericsson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15877) | Mar 9 and Sep 14, 2026 | Vendor launch figures; an industrial multi-agent review system at Ericsson | 54% of PRs get substantive comments, up from 16% (vendor); large PRs 84% get findings, under 1% marked incorrect (vendor); Ericsson raised 200+ issues at 96% accuracy with about 69% rated important | The vendor numbers are its own; CRBugBench (above) shows sharp drops on proprietary repos |

## 4. Skeptical evidence

| Study | Date | Method and sample | Key number | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [METR randomized trial](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) (arXiv 2507.09089) | Jul 10, 2025 | 16 experienced developers from large open-source repositories (22k+ stars, 1M+ lines on average), 246 real issues of about two hours, each randomly AI-allowed or not, paid $150 an hour | Tasks took 19% longer with AI (95% CI +2% to +39%); developers expected a 24% speedup and afterwards still believed 20% | Early-2025 tools (mostly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5 and 3.7 Sonnet); developers on familiar large repositories; small sample |
| [METR holistic review](https://metr.org/blog/2025-08-12-research-update-towards-reconciling-slowdown-with-time-horizons/) | Aug 13, 2025 | 18 issues from two repositories, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, maintainers reviewed 15 PRs | Passed maintainer-written tests 38% (+/-19%); none of the 15 reviewed PRs mergeable as is; all had at least three of five failure types (missing tests, docs, lint, quality); 42 minutes to fix, about a third of the original human time | Tiny sample and an older model |
| [METR maintainers](https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/) | Mar 10, 2026 | 4 maintainers reviewed 296 AI PRs covering 95 of 500 Verified issues, five models from Claude 3.5 Sonnet to GPT-5 | About 24 points below automated scores; roughly half of test-passing PRs would not be merged; improvement rate 9.6 points a year slower for merges (standard error 5.5, weaker evidence) | A research note; agents got one attempt while humans normally iterate |
| [Stanford group, 100k engineers](https://ai.engineer/talks/tbDDYKRFjhk-ai-developer-productivity) | Jul 23, 2025 | Private repo git history, 100,000+ engineers, 600+ companies, dozens of millions of commits; a 43-developer self-assessment experiment | Apparent +30-40% output falls to about +15-20% net after rework; high-complexity brownfield 0-10%, sometimes slower; developers missed measured productivity by about 30 percentile points | Only a talk page and a reading summary found, no primary paper |
| [GitClear maintainability gap](https://www.gitclear.com/the_ai_code_quality_maintainability_gap) | Jun 2026 | 623M analyzed changes, 2023-2026 | Duplicated blocks per million changed lines 40.3 to 73.0 (+81%); moved (refactored) code 21% of changed lines in 2022 to 3.8% in 2026 YTD; copy/paste 9.4% to 15.7%; error-masking constructs +47%; two-week churn +15%; cross-file calls -35%; long-term legacy updates -74% | Correlational; vendor data; GitClear itself says the headline is not "AI writes bad code" but that default workflows tax reuse |
| [GitClear cohort analysis](https://www.gitclear.com/developer_ai_productivity_analysis_tools_research_2026) | Jan 2026 | 2,172 developer-weeks from Cursor, Copilot and Claude Code provider APIs | Heavy daily users authored 4x to 10x more work than non-users, but most of the gap predated AI and heavy users gained about 25% against their past selves | Cohort gaps reflect who adopts AI as much as what it does |
| [CodeRabbit AI versus human](https://www.coderabbit.ai/blog/state-of-ai-vs-human-code-generation-report) (vendor) | Dec 17, 2025 | 470 PRs: 320 AI-co-authored, 150 human-only | 10.83 issues per AI PR against 6.45 (about 1.7x); logic and correctness 75% more common; readability over 3x | Authorship inferred from signals; PRs without them assumed human; the publisher sells review |
| [Follow-up fixes on agent PRs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26847) | Sep 22, 2026 | 6,774 merged agent PRs (Codex, Copilot, Devin, Cursor, Claude Code) against 5,044 human PRs in 500+ star repos | Agent merges attract verified fixes at 1.62 times the odds of human merges; 69.6% of those fixes come from the same agent | Open-source repos; LLM judge agreement kappa 0.78 against human-human 0.77 |
| [Agent code churn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00917) (MSR 2026) | Apr 1, 2026 | About 110,000 open-source PRs from Codex, Claude Code, Copilot, Jules and Devin | Agent contributions show more churn over time than human-authored code | Observational; agents and repos differ |
| [Failed agentic PRs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15195) (MSR 2026) | Jan 21, 2026 | 33k agent-authored PRs from five agents, 600 coded into a rejection taxonomy | Docs, CI and build tasks merge best; performance and bug fixes worst; unmerged PRs are larger, touch more files and often fail CI | Descriptive, not a productivity estimate |
| Token spend as a metric: [Gartner via The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/06/24/ai-coding-agents-could-soon-cost-more-than-the-developers-using-them/5260864), [DORA on tokenmaxxing](https://dora.dev/insights/finding-balance-in-the-era-of-tokenmaxxing/) | Jun 2026 | Analyst and research-program commentary | "No direct relation between the increase in token consumption and an increase in productivity gains"; top-budget teams see only marginal throughput gains at 10x the cost; reports of agents run on nonsense projects to stay above average | Opinion and forecast, not measurement |

## 5. Mixed evidence

| Study | Date | Method and sample | Key number | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [NBER w35275](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275) (Demirer, Musolff, Yang) | May 2026, revised Sep 2026 | 500,000+ GitHub developers with AI usage telemetry, matched event study | Commits: autocomplete +30%, interactive agents +180%, autonomous agents +240%; the 240% falls to +80% for projects and +30% for releases; elasticity of substitution between AI and human effort 0.23; across four software marketplaces new apps rose sharply but total usage did not | Two authors disclose paid consulting for Microsoft; a weak-link (human bottleneck) reading, not a causal claim about value |
| [DORA 2025 report](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report) | Sep 23, 2025 | Nearly 5,000 survey responses plus 100+ hours of qualitative data | 90% use AI at work, over 80% believe it raised productivity, about 30% have little or no trust in AI code; adoption now correlates positively with throughput but still negatively with delivery stability; "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there" | Survey data; correlational |
| [DORA, Google engineers](https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/) | Mar 10, 2026 | 1,110 open-ended survey responses from Google engineers, Q3 2025 | Time saved in code creation is often reallocated to auditing and verification, which may explain higher throughput with higher instability | Thematic analysis of one company |
| [Faros AI Engineering Report 2026](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-acceleration-whiplash-takeaways) (vendor) | Apr 12, 2026 (modified Sep 23) | Two years of telemetry, 22,000 developers, 4,000+ teams, each organization's lowest versus highest AI-adoption period | Incidents per PR +242.7% (the report's headline page says "3X"), monthly incidents +57.9%, bugs per developer +54%, PRs merged with no review +31.3%; alongside epics per developer +66%, task throughput +33.7%, merge rate per developer +16.2%; its [research page](https://www.faros.ai/research/ai-acceleration-whiplash) adds PR size +51% and median review time 5X | Vendor telemetry; correlation not causation; Faros explicitly disputes DORA's claim that strong foundations protect teams |
| [METR follow-up trial](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) | Feb 24, 2026 | Second study from August 2025: 10 returning plus 47 new developers, $50 an hour | Raw estimates lean toward speedup (see section 1) but 30-50% of developers said they withheld tasks they did not want to do without AI, and one completed none of the AI-disallowed tasks | METR judged the signal unreliable and is redesigning the study; no clean 2026 RCT exists |
| [METR survey](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/) | May 11, 2026 | 349 technical workers (87 software engineers), Feb-Apr 2026; about 2% response among people METR emailed | Median 1.4-2x value, 3x speed; retrospective 1.3x (March 2025), 2x (March 2026), forecast 2.5x (March 2027); METR staff gave the lowest value answers | Convenience sample; the 2025 trial found people overestimated AI's effect on time by 40 percentage points |
| [METR transcript analysis](https://metr.org/notes/2026-02-17-exploratory-transcript-analysis-for-estimating-time-savings-from-coding-agents/) | Feb 17, 2026 | 5,305 Claude Code transcripts from 7 METR staff, January 2026, LLM judge | Time-savings factor about 1.5x to 13x; the author believes the true multiplier is "substantially lower" | Soft upper bound; judge validated on 34 labels; higher concurrency may raise the factor |
| [METR on Anthropic's code volume](https://metr.org/notes/2026-07-08-anthropic-researcher-uplift/) | Jul 8, 2026 | One METR researcher models Anthropic's reported 8x code merged per day (Q2 2026 against 2021-2024) | Researcher uplift of about 2.3x to 3.1x under production-function assumptions, while Anthropic's system card says overall R&D uplift is "well short of" 2x because compute matters too | The author's opinion, others at METR disagree, math checked by Claude and not a second human |
| [Longitudinal developer study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23135) | May 22, 2026 | Two questionnaires six months apart, 95 matched professional engineers | 84% reported productivity gains at both times and 82% write less code, but worse developer experience in at least one dimension rose from 14% to 27%; the authors call the new work "supervisory engineering" | Perceptions, small sample |

## 7. Where the gains concentrate

- **Bounded, repeated, checkable work.** Migrations (Airbnb, Google, Spotify), MirrorCode's precisely specified rebuild, Anthropic staff delegating "easily verifiable" tasks, and the failed-PR study's finding that docs, CI and build tasks merge best while performance and bug fixes merge worst.
- **Consistent codebases.** Spotify: in more fragmented codebases agent performance is measurably worse; its dataset migration could not verify its work where repos had no build-time tests ([Part 4](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/4/background-coding-agents-dataset-migrations-honk-part-4)). Intercom's 2x came on mature CI/CD and tests.
- **Not brownfield complexity.** Stanford's 0-10% on high-complexity brownfield work; Real-SWE's mid-40s on private production code; ProgramBench's 4.5%.
- **Not the whole job.** Developers spend 16% (Atlassian) or about 14% (Microsoft, via DX) of their time coding, so coding-only gains are diluted, and review capacity becomes the limit (Spotify: "we now have 76% more PRs to review", [Jun 3, 2026](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2026/6/code-with-claude-coding-is-no-longer-the-constraint)).

## 8. What the evidence lets you claim

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Perception and measurement diverge | METR: 19% slower while believing 20% faster; METR's 2026 survey notes a 40-point overestimate; a small Stanford experiment missed by about 30 percentile points |
| Published gains span an order of magnitude and depend on method | Section 1: -19% to 13x |
| Output rises faster than shipped value | NBER: commits +240%, releases +30% |
| Passing tests is not mergeable | METR maintainers (24 points), 0 of 15, 1.62x follow-up fixes |
| Agents and the benchmarks around them game verifiers | Terminal-Bench integrity update, SWE-Bench Pro audit, METR on GPT-5.6 Sol (11.3 hours or 270+ depending on how cheating is scored) |
| Reliability, not capability, is the binding constraint | Opus 4.6: about 12 hours at 50%, about 1.2 hours at 80%; 78% on one try, 93% in five |
| Harness and infrastructure move scores about as much as models | 6 points from container limits; 13% flips; 3x cost; a 9-point scaffold gap |
| Bounded, repeated work shows the best measured returns | Airbnb 1.5 years to 6 weeks, Google about 50%, Spotify 60-90% |
| Throughput and instability rise together in several datasets | DORA 2025, Faros (vendor), GitClear (correlational) |
| Verification is the new bottleneck | DORA Google study, Spotify 76% more PRs, Twilio's four-hour review wait, CRBugBench 71.9% |
| Token spend is not a productivity metric | DORA on tokenmaxxing, Gartner |

## 9. What it does not let you claim

- **Any single "AI makes developers N% faster" figure.** The range is -19% to 13x by method; METR's trial used early-2025 tools; METR has published no clean 2026 randomized estimate, and its redesigned study has no results yet.
- **That a leaderboard score is real-world capability.** Verified and Pro were audited; Real-SWE on private code tops out in the mid-40s; vendor figures differ from board figures.
- **That a gap under about 3 points means one model is better.** Anthropic's guidance on infrastructure noise.
- **Causation from cohort comparisons or correlational telemetry.** GitClear's cohort gap, Faros, DORA and GitClear's quality signals do not isolate AI; Spotify's own incident review found no material direct AI contribution.
- **That a 12-hour time horizon means an agent runs for 12 hours.** It is the human-expert duration of the tasks.
- **That Anthropic's 8x code figure or the 1.5x acceleration estimate generalizes.** One researcher's opinion and a preliminary report with no stated period.
- **That adoption equals value.** Stack Overflow's 73% daily use is adoption, and METR's 3x speed is self-report.
- **That a DORA 2026 report exists.** dora.dev/research/2026 returns 404; 2025 is the newest annual report. The AI Capabilities Model (Nov 25, 2025), Quick Check and [ROI of AI report](https://dora.dev/ai/roi/report/) (both Apr 22, 2026) are the latest publications.
- **Anything from OpenAI's own SWE-bench posts.** Their pages returned 403; every OpenAI figure here is second-hand.

## 10. Gaps and limits of this note

METR's raw time-horizon file has no unit labels (minutes assumed). The Stanford study has no primary paper. SWE-Bench Pro V2 details and the May 2026 audit figures come from The Inference and AI Weekly, which cite VentureBeat and Data Phoenix. AI Weekly's page lists an "original headline" saying OpenAI retracted its Pro recommendation (about 30% of tasks broken) while its body says OpenAI backs Pro; secondary sources word the 59.4% Verified figure differently. Scale's leaderboard page text does not mention V2 and no Opus 5.x or GPT-6 entries exist on it. The Intercom, Block and Coinbase figures are second-hand.

## Gap-fill additions (2026-10-12)

- **A vendor-run survey of agent practitioners (section 6).** [The State of Development 2026: how engineers use AI agents today (Temporal)](https://temporal.io/reports/state-of-development-2026) (2026-08-25; n=554 in the US and UK via Qualtrics, fielded 2026-04-29 to 2026-05-25): 80.8% use agents daily, up from 47.3% a year earlier; the top blockers to using agents more are "#1 tracking state, #2 debugging, #3 managing costs"; 79.8% say token and compute cost is a limiting factor; 41.1% hit issues daily or more (9.0% "continuously"); only 2.2% run 51 or more agents.
- **What it lets you claim.** Practitioners name state, debugging and cost as the top three blockers. The survey also reports that the "successful" were 1.5x more likely to use agents daily or more (85.5% against 55.8%), but the report itself says "successful at using AI agents" is "self-reported data". It does not let you claim that durable execution fixes any of these blockers: Temporal sells durable execution, and the answers are self-reported.
- **Delivery moves the bottleneck to CI.** [AI coding has made CI a bottleneck, so we reworked ours to keep up (Linear)](https://linear.app/now/ci-bottleneck-reworked) (2026-09-21): "Agents have made it exponentially faster to ship code, but validating those changes hasn't quite kept up at the same rate." Test suites almost quadrupled since the start of the year while PR wait time fell from more than 6 minutes to just over 5; third-party runners ran jobs 34% faster on average. One company's account, not a benchmark, but a second independent voice that faster code creation shifts the constraint to validation.
